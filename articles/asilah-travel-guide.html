Asilah & Atlantic Coast

Asilah Travel Guide: Whitewashed Medina, Murals & Beaches (2026)

By Nadia Chraibi & TravelMoveMorocco Team
Updated March 2026
8 min read (1,520 words)

Asilah's 15th-century Portuguese ramparts framing the Atlantic breakers on Morocco's northern coastline.

Just 40 kilometers south of Tangier where the Mediterranean gives way to the pounding open rollers of the Atlantic Ocean, Asilah greets travelers with a rare, calming serenity. Unlike the sensory blitz of Marrakech or the labyrinthine complexity of Fes, this tiny seaside enclave is an oasis of gleaming whitewashed walls, cobalt blue shutters, and flower-draped wrought-iron balconies.

Originally a Phoenician settlement known as Zilis, Asilah was fortified by the Portuguese in 1471, who constructed its imposing honey-colored seawalls and watchtowers to safeguard spice trade routes. In 1978, local luminary Mohamed Benaissa and acclaimed painter Mohamed Melehi launched the International Cultural Moussem of Asilah, inviting master artists from across the globe to transform the blank medina walls into vibrant public canvases. Today, Asilah stands as Morocco's premier coastal art sanctuary.

Why Travelers Fall in Love with Asilah:
Asilah possesses none of the aggressive tout culture found in larger commercial hubs. Medina shopkeepers greet you with a peaceful nod, streets are impeccably clean, and the entire historical core is pedestrianized. It is the consummate retreat for slow travelers, beach lovers, and creative spirits seeking a restorative Atlantic interlude.

Top Sights & The Living Art Gallery

Asilah is small enough to navigate entirely on foot within a single afternoon, yet its understated coastal charm tempts visitors into staying for days.

1. The Annual Medina Murals

Every summer during the International Cultural Festival (July/August), the municipality coats the medina walls in a fresh layer of brilliant white lime wash. Renowned muralists and graffiti artists take up brushes, transforming residential alleyways into dynamic outdoor galleries.

  • Where to Find Them: Wander through the quiet residential streets near Derb Al Krikia and Rue Al Qahira. Murals range from bold geometric Moroccan tapestries to surrealist portraits and delicate Arabic calligraphy.
  • Ever-Changing Nature: Because walls are whitewashed and reimagined each summer, the art you photograph this year will be completely different on your next visit.

2. The Portuguese Ramparts & Borj Al-Kamra

Stroll along the robust 15th-century granite ramparts that have withstood five centuries of Atlantic gales and naval bombardments:

  • Borj Al-Kamra: The commanding square watchtower dominating the medina skyline near Bab Al-Kasbah, constructed during the Portuguese occupation to spot approaching Spanish and Ottoman fleets.
  • Mirador de Caraquia (Krikia Bastion): The southwestern corner bastion is Asilah's crown jewel. Here, stone embrasures jut out directly over crashing Atlantic waves, overlooking the whitewashed marabout dome of the Tomb of Sidi Ahmed Mansour. Come here at 6:30 PM: locals and travelers gather in companionable silence to watch the giant orange sun melt into the horizon.

3. Palais Raisuni (Palace of Raisuni)

Erected in 1909 by the infamous warlord, pirate, and kidnapper Pasha Moulay Ahmed er-Raisuni, this seaside palace is a masterwork of early 20th-century Moroccan-Andalusian craftsmanship. Featuring hand-carved cedar ceilings, delicate stucco arabesques, and a sea-facing loggia where Raisuni famously forced his prisoners to leap into the churning Atlantic rocks below. The palace is opened during festivals and cultural exhibits.

The Atlantic Beaches: Surf, Sand & Seclusion

While Asilah's town beach right by the port is convenient for a quick dip, the region's most magnificent coastal landscapes lie a few kilometers to the south.

1. Paradise Beach (Plage Rmilat)

Located 6 kilometers south of Asilah, Paradise Beach lives up to its moniker: a vast, golden crescent of fine sand flanked by towering red sandstone cliffs and pounding Atlantic surf.

  • How to Get There: For an unforgettable journey, hire a traditional two-wheeled horse-drawn carriage (calèche) from outside Bab Homar (approximately 150–200 MAD return, including waiting time). Alternatively, take a 15-minute grand taxi or 4x4 down the rugged dirt track.
  • Beach Life: Rustic driftwood and palm-thatch shacks set up in summer serving freshly caught sardines grilled over charcoal, sweet mint tea, and cold sodas. Bring sunscreen and plenty of cash; there are no ATMs on the coast.

2. Plage Sidi Mghait (Saint Mghait)

If Paradise Beach feels too social, venture 14 kilometers south to Sidi Mghait. Reached via an unpaved country track that winds through farmland and grazing sheep, this wild, windswept stretch of Atlantic coast is virtually deserted outside of July and August. It is a favored secret spot for independent surfers and sunset campers.

Where to Eat: Spanish-Moroccan Seafood & Tapas

Due to decades of Spanish protectorate influence and a thriving artisanal fishing harbor, Asilah's cuisine marries authentic northern Moroccan spices with Iberian seafood mastery.

Must-Try Regional Dishes

  • Fritura de Pescado (Fried Fish Platter): A generous heap of crispy, golden Atlantic delicacies—red mullet (rouget), tender calamari rings, baby sole, and sardines—seasoned with sea salt and fresh lemon wedges.
  • Seafood Tagine with Chermoula: Thick steaks of swordfish (espadon) or sea bass simmered in an earthen clay pot with green bell peppers, ripe tomatoes, garlic, preserved lemon, and pungent fresh coriander.
  • Fresh Churros Con Chocolate: In the early morning hours, follow your nose to the small fry carts near the municipal market outside Bab Homar. Golden, crispy spiral churros sprinkled with sugar and served piping hot in paper cones for just 5 MAD.

Handpicked Restaurants & Ocean Terraces

  • Casa Pepe (Plaza Zelaka): An Asilah institution operating since the 1980s. Celebrated for its Spanish-Moroccan menu featuring gambas al ajillo (sizzling garlic prawns), baked sea bream in rock salt, and authentic seafood paella.
  • Restaurant Port Gueydon: Perched just beside the seawall ramparts. Book an upper-level window table to enjoy Atlantic lobster, grilled swordfish, and chilled gazpacho while waves crash against the stone bastions below.
  • Restaurant Al Alba: Situated slightly outside the medina walls in a quiet neighborhood, this boutique hotel dining room offers refined Mediterranean-Moroccan fusion dishes, inventive seasonal salads, and homemade desserts in an intimate courtyard.
  • Restaurant La Perle d'Asilah: Friendly, unpretentious dining with exceptional daily catch specials, piping hot seafood pastilla, and generous hospitality.

Where to Stay: Coastal Riads & Eco-Lodges

Accommodations in Asilah emphasize breezy Atlantic light, terracotta tile floors, rooftop sea-view terraces, and tranquil Andalusian courtyards.

Top Accommodation Picks

  • Budget & Guesthouses ($30–$65/night):
    • Hotel Patio de la Luna: Located right opposite Bab Al-Kasbah, this cozy Spanish-style guesthouse features a tree-shaded patio and comfortable rooms at exceptional value.
    • Mia Hostels Asilah: A bright, clean, and social boutique hostel within walking distance of both the train station and the beach.
  • Mid-Range Boutique Riads ($75–$160/night):
    • Dar Al Alba: Elegantly appointed with northern Moroccan textiles, private hammam services, and a lush garden courtyard.
    • Riad Asilah: Centrally located inside the medina with high vaulted ceilings, authentic zellij tilework, and a panoramic rooftop terrace.
  • Luxury & Rural Retreats ($180–$320/night):
    • Berbari Guest House: Situated 10 km outside the town in rolling green countryside near bird sanctuaries. An eco-conscious farmhouse built from adobe and reclaimed timber, offering farm-to-table organic meals and total peace.

Practical Insider Tips for Asilah

Getting There & Rail Connectivity

Asilah is exceptionally easy to reach. The ONCF railway station is located 2 km north of town (a 20 MAD petit taxi ride into the medina). Direct regional trains connect Asilah to Tangier in just 35–45 minutes, making it an effortless first or final stop on a northern Morocco itinerary. You can also catch direct trains south to Rabat and Casablanca.

Seasonal Timing: Summer Buzz vs. Winter Serenity

  • July & August: The town comes alive with the Cultural Moussem, art workshops, and Moroccan vacationers from Casablanca and Madrid. Book accommodations months in advance.
  • May, June, September & October: The absolute sweet spot. Sunny 25°C (77°F) days, warm ocean breezes, empty beaches, and uncrowded art alleys.
  • Winter (December–February): Very quiet and romantic, but coastal humidity and ocean storms can bring brisk winds. Ensure your accommodation offers heating.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Asilah worth visiting on a day trip from Tangier?

Yes, absolutely! With the train taking only 40 minutes, a day trip to walk the ramparts, admire the murals, and eat grilled seafood at Casa Pepe is deeply rewarding. However, spending at least one night lets you enjoy the magical sunset from Krikia Bastion after the day tour groups return north.

Is swimming safe at Paradise Beach?

Atlantic currents and undertows can be strong along this stretch of coast. Swim where other locals are bathing, avoid venturing too far past the breaker line, and keep a watchful eye on children during high tide.

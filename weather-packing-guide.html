Morocco Weather & Packing Guide

Know what to expect month by month, and pack once, pack right, for medinas, mountains and the Sahara.

Four climates in one country

Morocco is not one weather forecast. In a single week you can wake up to Atlantic breezes, walk through a hot medina by noon and shiver in the Atlas by night. Pack for the regions you will visit, not just the country.

🌊

Coastal

Essaouira, Casablanca, Rabat and Asilah stay mild and breezy. Expect 18–27°C most of the year, with cool evenings and strong wind.

🏔️

Mountain

The High Atlas around Imlil and Toubkal is cold and changeable. Snow is common from November to April, and nights are chilly even in summer.

🏜️

Desert

Merzouga and the Draa Valley are scorching by day and cold at night, with swings of 20°C or more between afternoon and dawn.

🕌

Medina & inland cities

Marrakech and Fes are hot and dry in summer (35–40°C+) and surprisingly cool in winter. Narrow lanes trap heat, so plan breaks.

Season-by-season weather

Average daytime highs and nighttime lows are approximate and vary by region and year.

Spring · Mar–May

Best all-round season

18–28°C day · 8–15°C night

Green countryside, blooming valleys and comfortable city days. Book early, as this is peak season. Snow may linger on the Atlas peaks until April.

Summer · Jun–Aug

Coast yes, interior hot

30–42°C inland · 22–28°C coast

Head to Essaouira, Asilah or the mountains. Marrakech, Fes and the Sahara can exceed 40°C, so sightsee early and rest in the afternoon.

Autumn · Sep–Nov

Golden light, warm days

20–32°C day · 10–18°C night

Excellent for the desert, with cooler nights and clear skies. September is still hot inland, while November brings the first rain.

Winter · Dec–Feb

Cool, quiet, snowy peaks

12–20°C day · 2–8°C night

Sunny days in Marrakech but cold nights, with riads rarely heated well. Desert nights can drop near freezing, and the Atlas offers skiing near Oukaïmeden.

Complete Morocco packing checklist

Layers are the golden rule. Choose light, breathable fabrics that cover shoulders and knees.

👗 Medina-friendly clothing

  • Loose tops covering shoulders
  • Long skirts, light trousers or linen pants
  • Lightweight scarf or shawl
  • Breathable long-sleeve shirt
  • Swimwear for pools and beaches only

🧥 Atlas & cool-weather layers

  • Fleece or insulated mid-layer
  • Waterproof shell jacket
  • Warm hat and gloves (Nov–Apr)
  • Thermal base layer
  • Sturdy broken-in hiking boots

🐪 Desert gear

  • Cotton scarf or shesh for sand and sun
  • Sunglasses and wide-brim hat
  • Warm layer for freezing nights
  • Headlamp or torch
  • Reusable water bottle

🔌 Electronics & adapters

  • Type C and E plugs (230V, 50Hz)
  • Universal adapter and power bank
  • Offline maps and an eSIM
  • Phone dust and water protection
  • Camera with spare memory cards

🧴 Health & essentials

  • SPF 50 sunscreen and lip balm
  • Hand sanitiser and tissues
  • Stomach and rehydration remedies
  • Prescription medicines in original packaging
  • Travel insurance documents

🎒 Bags & documents

  • Passport valid 6+ months
  • Cross-body bag with zip closure
  • Small cash pouch for dirhams
  • Soft-sided bag or small wheeled case
  • Copies of bookings and insurance

Insider weather & packing tips

Respect local culture. Moroccans dress modestly, and covering shoulders and knees earns warmer welcomes, fewer stares and entry to more places. Beachwear belongs on the beach.
Protect yourself from the sun. Apply SPF 50 daily, wear a hat, and drink more water than you think you need. Carry a scarf, as it doubles as sun shield, dust mask and modesty cover.
Choose luggage for cobblestones. Medina lanes are bumpy, narrow and full of steps. A soft duffel or a backpack beats a hard suitcase, and many riads are a short walk from the nearest taxi drop-off.
Pack for night chills. Even in summer, desert and mountain evenings are cold. Bring one warm layer wherever you go.
Leave room to shop. Rugs, leather, ceramics and argan oil are hard to resist, so pack a foldable bag for the trip home.
